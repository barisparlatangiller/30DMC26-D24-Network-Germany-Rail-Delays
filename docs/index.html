Method and limits

PLAN. Long-distance timetable from gtfs.de (DELFI, CC BY 4.0), feed "latest-fv-free": every ICE, IC, EC, RJ, NJ/EN and partner train running on Monday 23 Nov 2026 between 06:00 and 18:00 (841 trips, 432 stations). Between stations the trains move along a smooth curve through their stops (centripetal Catmull-Rom) at constant speed, and stand still while they dwell. The curves are schematic, not the surveyed track.

WHAT IF. A small event simulation over all trains, in time order. A closed station holds every train due in the window until 09:00, then lets them leave 2 min apart. On every section A→B trains keep 3 min apart, so a late train delays those behind it. Late trains win back 4% of their running time and keep at most 1 min of each stop. A train set that ends its run turns around into the next train of the same kind leaving that station 15–90 min later (estimated: the feed has no vehicle data), and cannot leave earlier than 15 min after it arrived. When the Köln–Rhein/Main high-speed line is closed, trains on it detour via the Rhine valley (+45 min) and skip Montabaur and Limburg Süd.

It is a gentle model of how delays travel, not a forecast. Cancellations, reroutes, trains waiting for connections and crew changes are left out; real disruptions spread further.
